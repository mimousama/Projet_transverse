Email Phishing: How to Stay Safe

The Risk: Scammers send thousands of fake emails pretending to be a bank, a delivery company, or a government office. Their goal is to trick you into clicking a link to a fake website to steal your personal information or money.

How to spot a fake email:

  • It creates panic: They use urgent language like "Your account is locked" or "Immediate action required" to make you act without thinking.
  • It looks "almost" real: They copy company logos, but the email address is often strange (for example: taxes@gmail.com instead of an official .gov address).
  • It asks for payment: They might ask for a small "shipping fee" or "service fee" to get your credit card details.

What to do instead:

  • Stop and Breathe: If an email makes you feel panicked, that is a red flag. Take a moment to think.
  • Check the Address: Always click on the sender's name to see the full email address. If it doesn't match the company name, delete it.
  • Do Not Click Links: If you are worried, go to the official website by typing the address yourself or call the company using a number from your records.
  • Ignore Attachments: Do not open files or invoices you were not expecting.
