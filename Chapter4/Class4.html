AI Voice Cloning Scams

The risk: A scammer records a few seconds of your voice to clone it and uses artificial intelligence to convincingly impersonate you or your loved ones.

  • How it works: Scammers no longer need long conversations to steal your identity; three seconds of audio is currently the industry standard for advanced AI models to mimic a voice. They extract these audio samples from public social media profiles (like YouTube, TikTok, or Instagram), voicemail greetings, or by calling you from an unknown number just to get you to speak. The AI then analyzes your unique vocal patterns—such as pitch, tone, cadence, rhythm, and emphasis—to generate a realistic synthetic version of your speech. The criminal types whatever they want the person to say, and the AI generates an audio file that sounds exactly like them.

  • Common Scenarios: Fraudsters often target parents and grandparents by calling them and playing the cloned voice of a loved one in distress. By simulating fear, the scammer might claim the relative has been arrested or is in a terrible accident. Because the situation feels urgent, they press you to send money immediately via untraceable methods like wire transfers, cryptocurrency, or gift cards.

  • Spotting the Fake: Despite sounding highly realistic, AI voices sometimes have "tells." You might notice an unnatural 2-to-3-second pause after you say "hello" while the AI processes your voice, or you might realize the voice lacks natural breathing, verbal stumbles, or genuine emotional reciprocity when you interrupt.

How to protect yourself:

  • Do not answer calls from numbers you do not know. Allow these calls to go to voicemail. This simple habit gives you time to evaluate the situation logically without the immediate pressure of a live conversation.

  • Use call screening tools to manage incoming calls. You can activate automated assistants on modern smartphones or use third-party protection apps that warn you before you answer a potentially dangerous call. These features can act as a questionnaire, asking the caller to state their name and purpose, which is then redirected to your screen so you can safely verify their intent before picking up.

  • Establish a Family Safe Word: Create a unique code phrase with your family members. If someone calls claiming to be a relative in an emergency, ask them for the safe word or ask a question about an inside joke. Since the AI cannot access this private knowledge, it stops the scam immediately. If the caller cannot provide the code word, the best thing to do is end the call.

  • Hang up and verify: If you receive a suspicious call that seems to be from a relative, a bank, or the government, hang up immediately. Call the person or organization back directly using a trusted, known number. Scammers often use caller ID spoofing to make their fake calls appear legitimate on your screen, so you cannot trust the Caller ID.

  • Limit public audio: Limit what you share online, as scammers collect voice samples from social media videos. Ensure your personal posts are only visible to actual friends and family.

Be careful: Never share account numbers, Social Security numbers, passwords, PINs, or other sensitive information over the phone if you feel pressured.
