Correction: Quiz 2

Review your answers: Here are the correct answers and explanations based on the smishing lesson.

  • Question 1: Can a non-registered number on your phone send you a message with a pre-set name?
    Correct answer: It can happen, even more from fraudsters.
    Explanation: The hacker uses computer tools to mask their number and display a known name, such as "Netflix", "Postal Service", or "ANTAI". The goal is to make you leave the secure environment of your phone.

  • Question 2: What should you do if you receive an SMS asking you to click a link?
    Correct answer: Use a free link checker on Internet.
    Explanation: You must absolutely not click on any link received via SMS. Instead, check if they are trustworthy with a free link checker on Internet or by calling the company directly with a known number.

  • Question 3: What could you also do if you receive an SMS asking you to click a link from your bank for example?
    Correct answer: Contact the sender via another method like a trusted email or a rendezvous.
    Explanation: You should not respond to SMS messages requesting urgent action or payment. Calling the company directly with a known number is the safest way to check the information.

  • Question 4: What is the main risk of clicking on a link received via SMS?
    Correct answer: You might be redirected to a fake website that steals your personal information or infected with a malware.
    Explanation: The goal is to lure you to a web page that they control, which might look exactly like an official website. By interacting with it, you are actually giving your banking "key" to the thief.

  • Question 5: What should you do if you receive an SMS from a unknown sender?
    Correct answer: Do not respond and delete the message.
    Explanation: You must not respond to SMS messages requesting urgent action or payment, and do not call back unknown numbers that leave you vague messages asking you to contact them immediately.

Be careful: a similar email or one using the name of a relative may also be used.
